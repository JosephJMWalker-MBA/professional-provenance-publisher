## Core scientific-AI risk

The risk is not limited to hallucination or alteration of raw files.

When AI can access both measurements and research notes, ordinary operations can change the evidentiary meaning of the dataset:

- cleaning;
- outlier exclusion;
- labeling;
- metadata inference;
- schema conversion;
- experiment matching;
- normalization;
- note summarization;
- relevance selection; and
- hypothesis ranking.

A note is evidence that a scientist wrote the note. The interpretation inside the note is not automatically evidence that the phenomenon occurred.

An AI classification is evidence that a model produced that classification. It is not automatically evidence that the classified condition was real.

The architecture should therefore make **evidence status** explicit and prevent AI-generated interpretation from silently being promoted into the evidentiary layer.

## Recommended first conversation

The first meeting should test the hypothesis rather than pitch a fixed implementation.

Questions to resolve:

1. Where does AI currently touch, or soon expect to touch, R&D measurements and research notes?
2. Which transformations are deterministic today, and which rely on human or probabilistic judgment?
3. How are source records, transformations, exclusions, labels, and later interpretations currently traced?
4. Who owns scientific-data governance when AI is involved: R&D, AI Technology, Enterprise Data, Responsible AI, or a shared function?
5. Has Sherwin-Williams already identified recursive-summary / interpretation-as-evidence risk?
6. What would a narrow pilot look like that can measure productivity upside and false-signal risk without disrupting the scientific process?
7. If the architecture proves valuable internally, is any element protectable or commercially useful without weakening Sherwin-Williams’ competitive advantage?
